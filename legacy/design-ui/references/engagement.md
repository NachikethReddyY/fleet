# Engagement that supports progress

Use this reference when the product genuinely needs repeated participation. Start with the user's real-world outcome and a session's done state. Treat the supplied psychology notes as design hypotheses, not established causal evidence or a requirement to maximize app opens.

## Predictable progress and controlled surprise

The user's "craving machine" notes describe variable-ratio reinforcement and unpredictable next rewards. Keep earned progress and reward rules transparent: show the action, its credit, the next milestone, and when credit is recorded. Optional surprise gifts can add delight without changing promised rewards or requiring repeated checking. Never make core success depend on a hidden random schedule.

Specify the loop as cue, useful action, feedback, durable progress, and an explicit stopping point. Measure completed useful actions and user-reported value before return frequency. Audit each screen for a done state instead of manufacturing an endless queue.

## The long game

Streaks should compound into durable achievements, skills, or a meaningful history. Define missed-day handling, time zone boundaries, grace or pause behavior, and recovery. A missed day must not silently erase unrelated earned progress.

Levels and monthly challenges can reset a seasonal score while preserving lifetime accomplishments. Show exactly what resets and when. Keep personal records distinct from temporary competition. Agree on these rules before implementing stateful logic.

## The visible scoreboard

Social comparison, leaderboards, callouts, and visible achievements are candidate mechanisms for community and identity. Tie metrics to something users value and can influence. Offer comparison with one's own past as well as optional, relevant peer groups. Define scoring, ties, season boundaries, and participation rules.

Support real human connection through chosen teams, shared goals, and recognition. Let users control whether achievements and metrics are public. A metric should reflect progress, not assign personal worth. Identity and community are reasons to build lasting value, not to make leaving irreversible; preserve control over participation and notification settings.

## Urgency and unfinished tasks

Countdowns can represent a real event or an explicitly chosen challenge. State the deadline, consequence, and recovery before entry. Reminders should follow user-selected timing and frequency, with quiet hours, pause, and opt-out. Updates should describe material changes rather than repeatedly demand attention.

The notes invoke loss aversion and the Zeigarnik effect to explain returning to unfinished tasks. Do not assert that either guarantees retention. Review for manufactured fear, hidden penalties, repeated notifications, and pressure to check without a useful action. Prefer an honest progress summary and a clear next session over "you should be scared not to open the app."

## Decision gate

For each proposed mechanic, record the user benefit, visible rules, stored progress, reset/loss behavior, social visibility, notification control, and done state. Test earning credit, missing a session, crossing a period boundary, returning after absence, and disabling reminders. Ship only the mechanics within the agreed product scope. Retention effects remain unverified until observed with users.
