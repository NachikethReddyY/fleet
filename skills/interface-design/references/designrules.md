# Design Rules: Earn Every Treatment

Models often overuse cards, pills, huge titles, tiny support text, gradients, blur, and decoration. Use **earned styling**: every treatment improves hierarchy, grouping, state, identity, comprehension, or action.

## Derive from product

Establish user, task, context, personality, and density before visual language. Start with content on the shared canvas. Choose dark, glassy, neon, rounded, editorial, or other strong treatments only when they fit accepted product evidence.

Keep content distinct from controls. Materials, translucency, and depth should clarify ownership or layering; appearance alone is insufficient.

## Restraint

A container earns a boundary when it is independently actionable, groups content that must be understood together, has distinct ownership or state, or represents a real layer. Prefer spacing, alignment, and typography before rectangles. Nested boundaries need different jobs at each level.

Reserve capsules for compact semantics: status, tag, filter, selection, short token, or a control whose shape belongs to the system. Render ordinary headings, names, descriptions, dates, and counts as text.

Anchor type in readable body and controls. Scale headings for their real role and available space. Build contrast with weight, color, line height, placement, and space before extreme size. Immediate tasks should remain visible in the initial composition.

Create emphasis by quieting competition. Most task views need one primary action; a second prominent action needs a clear reason. Severity does not automatically make a destructive action primary.

Give gradients, glows, patterns, illustration, motion, and icons named roles. Keep one dominant motif. Frequent actions should feel immediate, not performative.

Choose cards, tables, tabs, charts, sidebars, timelines, and diagrams only when content has the relationship they represent. Fewer patterns can be the more complete design.

Number items only when order means something. Labels and headline accents must communicate structure or meaning; omit those added solely to make a composition look designed.

## Restraint audit

1. **Rectangles:** remove each border, fill, radius, and shadow; restore only cues needed for grouping, action, ownership, or layer.
2. **Capsules:** classify each as status, identity, selection, filter, or action; render the rest as text.
3. **Type:** compare largest heading, body, controls, labels, and metadata at narrow and wide sizes; reconnect extremes.
4. **Decoration:** name each treatment's role; remove repeated or unowned roles.
5. **Icons:** remove symbols that repeat nearby text without improving recognition or scanning.
6. **Patterns:** verify each component matches a real content or interaction relationship.
7. **Signals:** simplify elements carrying several cues until each remaining cue communicates a different fact.
8. **Deletion:** keep the quieter result whenever meaning and usability remain.

Audit passes when the primary content is obvious, support remains readable, every treatment has a job, and further removal would reduce understanding or use.
