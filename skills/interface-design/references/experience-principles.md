# Experience Principles

Read this for every interface build or review. Apple's most portable strength is disciplined product judgment, not Apple-looking surfaces. Apply these principles to websites and other platforms through their own conventions.

## Purpose

Name the user, immediate job, and useful outcome. Give the core task disproportionate care. Start with content and action; remove features or elements that do not strengthen the outcome. Study familiar solutions to learn expectations, then solve this product's problem.

## Agency and responsibility

Take users directly to their work. Guided flows remain escapable and skip unnecessary steps. Make consequences predictable; provide cancel, undo, retry, or recovery in proportion to cost. Preserve work through errors and interruption.

Request sensitive data or permission only when its value is clear. Explain use at the moment of need, respect refusal, and protect destructive, financial, private, or externally visible actions. Visual hierarchy must support free choice rather than manipulation.

## Familiarity and flexibility

Prefer platform, browser, and domain conventions. Similar-looking elements behave alike; the same action keeps its name and feedback. Novel presentation earns its cost only when it improves a named task while preserving semantics, state, input, and recovery.

Assume varied ability, language, viewport, theme, posture, viewing distance, and input. Shared identity does not require identical composition. Adapt density, navigation, and feedback while preserving purpose, recognizable relationships, and task state. Treat viewport and input as independent.

## Simplicity, craft, and delight

Simplicity removes friction, not necessary context. Make one starting point clear, reveal secondary material progressively with a visible cue, and let content dominate its controls.

Craft includes clear writing, reliable behavior, performance, accessibility, real states, and precision at target size. Prototype early with representative content and test under actual constraints.

Choose an appropriate emotion—confidence, calm, energy, warmth, play—and express it through useful feedback, recovery, motion, language, and detail. Delight follows care; decoration cannot substitute for it.

## Hierarchy, harmony, consistency

- **Hierarchy:** reading order, prominence, and action priority reveal what matters now.
- **Harmony:** form and behavior suit product purpose, platform, environment, and input.
- **Consistency:** semantics, language, placement, and feedback stay predictable across contexts.

Use native web semantics and browser behavior for websites. Translate platform guidance by principle; never copy Apple dimensions, materials, or components as universal web standards.

## Gate

- User, job, context, and outcome are explicit.
- Core work is direct, understandable, and free of traps.
- Consequential actions preserve agency, privacy, and recovery.
- Familiar patterns keep their behavioral contracts.
- Adaptation preserves content, relationships, and state.
- Hierarchy exposes one clear starting point without hiding required actions.
- Every element contributes meaning, action, state, grouping, or identity.
- Accessibility, language, performance, and non-ideal states receive full care.
- Intended emotion comes from the whole experience.

Portable principles are derived from Apple's [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles); platform-specific projects must still follow their current native guidance.
